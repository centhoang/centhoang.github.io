Privacy policy

HandyDrive · Last updated 11 November 2026

In short: HandyDrive runs on your PC and talks only to your own Google Drive. The developer runs no server, has no access to your files or your Google account, and collects no data about you.

About HandyDrive

HandyDrive is a Windows application (a command-line tool, hgd, a tray app, handydrive, and an Explorer menu extension). It parks git repositories and syncs files and folders to Google Drive. It is a personal project developed by centhoang.

You connect HandyDrive to your own Google account using a Google Cloud OAuth client that you create. Everything the app does happens between your PC and your Google Drive.

What the developer collects

Nothing. HandyDrive has no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no update check. It never sends data to the developer or to any third party. The only services it contacts are Google (see below) and, through the git command, your own git host.

Google user data

Permission requested

When you sign in, HandyDrive requests a single Google OAuth scope:

https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file

This scope lets the app see and modify only the files and folders that HandyDrive itself created in your Drive. It does not give access to your other Drive files, and HandyDrive does not request your name, email address, profile, contacts or any other Google data.

What HandyDrive stores in your Drive

Everything goes in a HandyDrive folder in My Drive:

  • An encrypted repository (Storage (encrypted)): the contents of the items you park or sync, encrypted on your PC before they are uploaded. File names and contents are not readable from Drive without your recovery key.
  • A readable text note per item, so you can see what is stored. These notes are not encrypted. A synced item's note holds its name, its full path on your PC, whether it is a file or folder, its size and file count, its state, when it was last synced, your computer's name, and a short snapshot id. A parked repo's note holds its name, original path, git remote URL, branch, size and the time it was parked.

Anyone who can open your Google Drive can read those notes, so don't share the HandyDrive folder if you don't want them to see item names and paths.

How the data is used

HandyDrive uses this access only to provide its features: uploading what you park or sync, listing and restoring it, and deleting it when you ask. It does not use the data for advertising, profiling, analytics or to train any model, and no person other than you reads it.

Sharing and transfer

HandyDrive does not share, sell or transfer your data to anyone. The app contacts only these Google services, and only when you set up, sign in or use it: accounts.google.com (sign-in), oauth2.googleapis.com (tokens) and www.googleapis.com (the Drive API).

Google API Services User Data Policy

HandyDrive's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

These stay on your PC. Your recovery key is never sent to Google or to the developer, which means nobody can recover it for you: if you lose it and your saved copy, the encrypted data in your Drive can't be read.

Git and your repositories

Parking and unparking run the git command on your PC. Its checks, git clone on unpark and any other git network traffic go between you and your own git host (such as GitHub), using your existing git setup. HandyDrive never asks for or handles your git credentials.

When it parks a repo, HandyDrive records the repo's remote URL exactly as git reports it (in the encrypted manifest and in the readable note described above). If your remote URL has a password or token written into it, that would be recorded too, so keep credentials out of remote URLs.

Removing your data

You are in control of everything HandyDrive stores:

  • Stop syncing an item and delete its cloud copy with hgd unsync (or Un-sync in the menu). Restore cloud-only items first if you still need them.
  • Delete the HandyDrive folder in Google Drive to remove the encrypted repository and all notes. This destroys your parked and cloud-only data (Google keeps deleted files in its trash for 30 days unless you empty it), so restore anything you still need first.
  • Revoke HandyDrive's access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Remove local data by deleting the %APPDATA%\HandyDrive folder and the two entries ending in .HandyDrive in Windows Credential Manager.

Because the developer holds none of your data, there is nothing to delete on the developer's side.
